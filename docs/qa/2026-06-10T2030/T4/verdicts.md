## Journey verdict: pass-with-friction

Generation failed silently from the user's perspective — the transition from start card to Q1 happened with no perceivable loading feedback, and the error banner appeared alongside Q1 with no warning period. The failure message surfaces an internal provider name ("groq") and technical concepts ("key", "region") that a non-technical user cannot act on. The recovery mechanism works correctly: the banner's second sentence clearly states the test will use sample items, and the fallback test runs to completion without any functional issues. All five questions rendered correctly, selection highlighting was consistent (dark border = selected, greyed Next = no selection, dark Next = ready), and the final screen appropriately replaced "Next" with "See results".

## Findings

- [high] No perceivable loading/generating state after clicking "Generate a fresh test with AI" — the app transitions directly from the start card to Q1 with the error banner already visible, giving the user no indication that generation was ever attempted before failing. Steps 2, shots/step-02-before.png → shots/step-02-after.png.
- [high] Failure message exposes internal technical details to the user: "Access denied by 'groq'. Your key may be invalid, revoked, or the provider may be blocking your region." The word "groq", "key", and "region" are meaningless to a non-technical user and may cause alarm or confusion. Step 2, shots/step-02-after.png.
- [med] The error banner is persistent across all 5 questions (visible in every screenshot from step 2 onward), occupying prime top-of-card real estate for the entire test duration. A user who understands the fallback is happening is forced to read the error notice on every question for the full session. Steps 2–9, all shots from step-02-after.png through step-09-after.png.
- [low] Step 8 selection state (Option A, Q4) was not visible in the after-screenshot despite domMutations confirming the click registered. This is likely a screenshot-timing artifact rather than a genuine render gap, but a user watching closely could momentarily believe their click was missed. Step 8, shots/step-08-after.png.
